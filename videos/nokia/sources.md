# How Nokia Lost the Phone Market in Under 6 Years: research brief

## Brief

- **What:** Nokia went from selling 4 in 10 of the world's phones to selling
  its phone business to Microsoft.
- **When:** Peak share in Q4 2007, sale announced September 2013.
- **Where:** Nokia is headquartered in Espoo, Finland.
- **Who:**
  - Nokia
  - Apple (iPhone)
  - Nokia CEO Stephen Elop (joined 2010, moved to Microsoft at the sale)
  - Microsoft
- **Cause:** Touchscreen smartphones (iPhone from 2007, plus Android, both named
  in Elop's own memo) took over the market Nokia led.
- **Turning point:** In February 2011, Elop's "burning platform" memo told staff
  the company had to change or perish. Three days later Nokia announced it would
  bet its smartphones on Microsoft's Windows Phone.
- **Outcome:**
  - Nokia's share kept falling. It lost the #1 spot to Samsung in 2012.
  - In September 2013 it agreed to sell its Devices & Services business to
    Microsoft (€5.44B deal including patent licenses).
  - In July 2015 Microsoft took a ~$7.6B impairment on that business and cut up
    to 7,800 jobs.
  - Nokia survived as a network-infrastructure company. It bought
    Alcatel-Lucent, completed in 2016.

## Notes and caveats

- **The title overstates two things:**
  - "To nothing": Nokia still exists as a networks company. Only the phone
    business went.
  - "5 years": peak (Q4 2007) to sale announcement (Sep 2013) is about
    5 years 9 months.

  The narration avoids both claims. The approved title (option B) is "How Nokia
  Lost the Phone Market in Under 6 Years".
- The narration doesn't claim **why** management chose Windows Phone, or that
  the choice "caused" the decline. It states the sequence of documented events.
- On-screen people are **generic toon characters**, not likenesses. Elop is
  referred to only as "Nokia's new CEO", and only for documented actions (the
  memo, the partnership, moving to Microsoft).
